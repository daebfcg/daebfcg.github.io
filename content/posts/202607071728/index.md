## Starting Point

This guitar neck starts out as a rough 1x2.25 +/- timber of black cherry _(Prunus serotina)_.

{{<imgresize 00_blank.jpg "800x800" "Neck blank">}}

First it is planed flat on all sides.

{{<imgresize 01_planing.jpg "800x800" "Planing">}}

Planing reveals that it is mostly quartered and has nice medullary rays showing.

{{<imgresize 02_planed.jpg "800x800" "Planed surface">}}

{{<imgresize 03_planed.jpg "800x800" "Planed surface - other end">}}

## Plan

The timber is long enough so that the off-cut from the end can be used to build up the heel (stacked heel) with enough length for the body-joint tenon.

I am shooting for a 2 inch body depth, so the heel will need to be close to 2 inches deep.  The heel cap will make up any difference.  The tenon needs to be at least 2.625" long to get a tight and wiggle-free neck joint.  The headstock in rough form needs to be at least 7 to 7.5 inches long.  Just enough length here to make the whole neck, heel, and headstock.

{{<imgresize 04_layout.jpg "800x800" "Planning the neck">}}

At this stage, the location of the 0-fret is determined.  This is the key position from which all other measurements are made:

- the nut and headstock-to-neck break-angle point
- enough length left in the headstock
- the 14th fret neck-to-body joint
- the 2.625 inch tenon end point
- enough remaining off-cut for the stacked-heel
- enough remaining off-cut for the headstock side extensions (ears)

## Scarf Joint

Starting with the headstock end, the cut for the scarf joint is made.  This provides the angle for the headstock by flipping the cutoff part over and rejoining.

For the Super K guitar, the headstock angle is 10 degrees.  This is shallower than is typical, but works out fine with the thinner body.

{{<imgresize 05_scarf_angle.jpg "800x800" "Angle for the scarf joint cut">}}

This is cut free-hand on the band saw, and then the mating surfaces cleaned up on the disk sander.

{{<imgresize 06_scarf_cut.jpg "800x800" "Scarf joint parts">}}

A scarf joint always wants to slide apart from the clamping pressure during gluing, so each end needs to be anchored securely against double-clamped blocks of wood on the bench.

{{<imgresize 07_scarf_glue.jpg "800x800" "Scarf joint glue up anchored">}}

Four strong C-clamps plus one bar clamp are used to tightly close up the hide-glue joint.

{{<imgresize 08_scarf_glue.jpg "800x800" "Scarf joint glue up clamps">}}

## Truss Rod

Now that the headstock angle joint is made, the truss rod and reinforcement bars can be installed.

I am using an aluminum U-channel truss rod and 2 carbon fiber bars this go-round.  The aluminum is lighter, although a bit wider, than the hot rod type used on Super K 1.

{{<imgresize 09_truss_plan.jpg "800x800" "Truss rod plan">}}

The workpiece is held in the big vise for routing.

{{<imgresize 10_pre_route.jpg "800x800" "Neck ready for routing">}}

Channels routed.

{{<imgresize 11_routed.jpg "800x800" "Channels for truss rod and carbon fiber bars">}}

The truss rod and CF bars get a strip of spruce CA-glued to the top of them.  This is planed flush on the sides, and adds some height that can be planed off later.  The extra height helps in clamping them in during gluing.  More importantly, it buries the metal and CF components beneath some wood so that the face of the neck can be further planed and scraped before the fretboard is glued down.

{{<imgresize 12_truss_prep.jpg "800x800" "Truss rod and cd bars prepped for installation">}}

I am using the gray JB Weld epoxy to adhere the aluminum to the wood.  Just the bare minimum amount needed.  The CF bars can be set in with hide glue.

{{<imgresize 13_epoxy_truss.jpg "800x800" "Epoxy laid in">}}

{{<imgresize 14_truss_glue_up.jpg "800x800" "Gluing in the truss rod and CF bars">}}

The extra spruce topper pieces are then planed flush with the neck surface.

{{<imgresize 15_face_plane.jpg "800x800" "Truss rod and CT bars installed">}}

Note that the CF bars extend into the headstock, providing extra rigidity in this area.

{{<imgresize 14_truss_installed.jpg "800x800" "Truss rod and CF bars at headstock">}}

## Heel

Next, the heel end is cut off 3 inches past the 14th fret body-joint line, and stacked under to form the heel.  The extra 3 inches will form the tenon.

{{<imgresize 15_heel_glue_up.jpg "800x800" "Stacked heel glue up">}}

{{<imgresize 16_heel_glue_up.jpg "800x800" "Stacked heel glue up">}}

Next a rough heel cap is glued to the bottom.  This is Persimmon, matching the fretboard and tailpiece stock.

{{<imgresize 17_heel_cap.jpg "800x800" "Heel cap">}}

The extra length here is preserved for the headstock ears...

## Tenon

Now the body joint and tenon are cut out.  The neck is set at a 3.5 degree angle to the body.  So the vertical cut for the body joint needs to be at this angle.  The cuts leave a 1 inch wide tenon that will extend into the mortice cut into the headblock in the body.

{{<imgresize 19_tennon_plan.jpg "800x800" "Mapping out the tenon">}}

{{<imgresize 18_tennon_plan.jpg "800x800" "Angle for the body joint">}}

{{<imgresize 22_tennon_plan.jpg "800x800" "Tenon cut lines">}}

I clamped down a machinists square to guide my saw on this critical angle.

{{<imgresize 20_tennon_end_cut.jpg "800x800" "Cutting the tenon ends">}}

The side cuts are made with a Douzuki dovetail saw, leaving the 1 inch wide tenon in the center.

{{<imgresize 21_tennon_end_cut.jpg "800x800" "Cutting the tenon ends">}}

The sides of the tenon are then sliced out on the bandsaw.  These side waste pieces are reserved for the headstock ears.

{{<imgresize 23_tennon_sides_cut.jpg "800x800" "Cutting the tenon sides">}}

And there is the rough tenon.

{{<imgresize 24_tennon_cut_out.jpg "800x800" "Rough tenon cut out">}}

The inner angles are cleaned up with a chisel.

{{<imgresize 25_tennon_clean_up.jpg "800x800" "Tenon clean up">}}

Now the tenon is trimmed to 2.625 inches long, also at the same 3.5 degree angle.

The horizontal cut line angles are also mapped out.  This will leave the top of tenon on the same plane as the top of the guitar body.  The extra heel cap wood will all be trimmed away once it is matched up with the body.

{{<imgresize 28_tennon_done.jpg "800x800" "Tenon done">}}

## Headstock

Now to attach the ears.  These are glued on with care being taken so that they come up just slightly proud of the face of the headstock (to leave room for planing down).  A short riser is placed under the headstock (face down) for this process to achieve this.

{{<imgresize 26_ears_glue_up.jpg "800x800" "Headstock ears glue up">}}

With the ears glued on, the headstock can be planed flat in preparation for the headplate to be attached.  A small block plane works great here.

The headplate will cover up all scarf joint and ear glue lines when viewed from the front.

{{<imgresize 27_headstock_planed.jpg "800x800" "Headstock planed flat">}}

## Done for now

The major neck assembly phase is now done.  Next up is making the headplate lamination and gluing it down, and then the final headstock shaping.
